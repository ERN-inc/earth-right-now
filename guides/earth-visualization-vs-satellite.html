Is a live Earth visualization a satellite camera?

Not necessarily. A live satellite camera would capture actual imagery from a sensor in orbit. A visualization can instead combine maps, imagery, animation and time-based events to present an understandable view of Earth.

EARTH RIGHT NOW is a live broadcast of an Earth visualization, not a continuous real-time satellite camera feed. Its scenes and city events are designed to help viewers experience the rhythm of day and night around the world.

For operational weather warnings, precise astronomical times or scientific satellite observations, use the relevant official meteorological, astronomical or space-agency data source. A visualization is valuable for exploration but should not be mistaken for a measurement instrument.

How to tell the difference

Satellite imagery is captured by sensors in space and may be updated at intervals rather than continuously. Some services provide recent images with a processing delay. Live camera footage comes from a camera transmitting actual views, but it covers only what that camera can see. Earth visualizations use graphical representations to explain processes such as the movement of the day-night boundary.

Which should you use?

For a geography lesson on Earth’s rotation, compare the global sunrise and sunset cycle and daylight in Jakarta and Reykjavík. For cloud imagery, storms or other measured conditions, consult an official satellite or weather provider. For a free ambient Earth display, see the browser display guide.

Frequently asked questions

Does “live Earth” always mean real-time satellite video?

No. “Live” can describe a currently running broadcast of a visualization. Check the source and description to learn whether the images are measurements, recordings or rendered scenes.

Can a visualization show the exact weather outside my house?

Not by itself. A visualization is not a substitute for a location-specific forecast or live observation.
