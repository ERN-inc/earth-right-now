Why sunrise and sunset happen at different times

Earth rotates once approximately every 24 hours, so locations at different longitudes face the Sun at different moments. The transition between daylight and darkness moves across the planet as Earth turns.

Latitude and season also matter. Near the equator, daylight length varies relatively little through the year. Farther from the equator, summer days become longer and winter days shorter; near the poles, daylight can last nearly all day or disappear for extended periods.

A city's official sunrise time also depends on its time zone and local daylight-saving rules. Comparing clock times alone can be misleading: two cities can experience similar sunlight conditions while showing different local times.
