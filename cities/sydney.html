Sunrise, sunset and daylight in Sydney

Sydney, Australia, lies near 33.9° S. Because it is south of the equator, its longest days arrive around the December solstice and its shortest days around the June solstice. That is the opposite seasonal pattern from northern cities such as Tokyo and London.

Summer evenings and winter mornings

During Sydney's summer, the Sun spends more time above the horizon and daylight stretches into the evening. During winter, the Sun follows a lower, shorter path across the sky. Day length changes gradually throughout the year, with the fastest changes generally near the equinoxes.

What daylight saving changes

Sydney observes Australian Eastern Standard Time (UTC+10) in winter and Australian Eastern Daylight Time (UTC+11) during its daylight-saving period. Moving the clock forward makes sunrise and sunset appear one hour later by local clock time; it does not alter the Sun's position. Not every Australian state follows the same daylight-saving rules.

Compare the hemispheres

Compare Tokyo or Seoul with Sydney to see how northern and southern seasons differ. Singapore, near the equator, has a much smaller seasonal change in day length. For the science behind these differences, read why sunrise times change.
