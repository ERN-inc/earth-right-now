Explore daylight around the world

Sunrise and sunset change with latitude, season and time zone. Start with these city guides, each explaining a different part of the planet's daily light cycle. These are educational guides, not real-time astronomical tables.

  • Tokyo, Japan — Japan Standard Time and northern mid-latitude seasons
  • Jakarta, Indonesia — tropical daylight close to the equator
  • Singapore — near-equatorial daylight and UTC+8 solar time
  • Seoul, South Korea — mid-latitude seasons and Korea Standard Time
  • Sydney, Australia — southern-hemisphere seasons and daylight saving
  • Mumbai, India — tropical monsoon daylight and half-hour time offset
  • Cape Town, South Africa — southern seasons and mountain horizons
  • Reykjavík, Iceland — high-latitude summer twilight and winter darkness
  • Dubai, United Arab Emirates — Gulf Standard Time and desert daylight
  • Buenos Aires, Argentina — southern seasons and Argentina Time
  • London, United Kingdom — northern latitude and daylight saving time
  • New York, United States — Eastern Time and seasonal daylight

Compare daylight across continents

Near the equator: Jakarta and Singapore offer a useful starting point for understanding why daylight length changes relatively little during the year. Compare them with Reykjavík, where summer and winter daylight differ dramatically.

Opposite seasons: Compare Tokyo with Sydney and Cape Town. Northern and southern hemispheres experience opposite seasonal daylight patterns.

Different local clocks: London, New York, Mumbai and Dubai show why time zones and daylight-saving rules must be distinguished from the Earth's actual daylight cycle.
