Sunrise, sunset and daylight in Seoul

Seoul, South Korea, sits near 37.6° N, a mid-northern latitude with noticeable seasonal differences in daylight. The Sun rises earlier and sets later during the northern summer than during winter.

What changes between summer and winter?

Near the June solstice, Seoul experiences long days and comparatively short nights. Near the December solstice, daylight is shorter and the Sun follows a lower arc across the sky. The transition is gradual across spring and autumn, not a sudden change at the solstice.

Korea Standard Time and solar time

Seoul uses Korea Standard Time (UTC+9) year-round, with no current daylight saving time. Its longitude is about 127° E, west of the time zone's nominal 135° E central meridian. This means clock noon and the Sun's highest point do not generally coincide; the equation of time adds a smaller seasonal variation.
